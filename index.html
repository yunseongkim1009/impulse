Impulse

Crumple zones for drones that fly low over people.

OverviewImpulse lattice

Impulse is a 3D-printed lattice that forms a drone's propeller guards and underside. When the drone falls, it squashes and absorbs the hit, in the lowest few meters where a parachute has no room to open.

01–02 · Airframe

Built around your drone.

Each lattice is designed for one airframe. Its mass, arm layout and how it tends to land all change the design.

03 · Propellers

The parts that cut.

Spinning props are the obvious injury risk on a small drone, and the first thing a guard has to deal with.

04 · Lattice prop guard

A guard that also pads.

In a tumbling fall the rim may hit before the belly, so we design it as padding too. Which part hits first is one of the things we measure.

05 · Belly lattice

A belly built to crush.

Under the frame, a lattice that collapses in a controlled way, so the impact is spread over more time and distance.

06 · Replaceable section

Crush a section. Swap a section.

A hard landing can flatten one area. Each tile clips out on its own, so you replace one tile instead of reprinting the whole part.

01–06 · Assembled

One printed system.

Guards and belly fit the drone as one set of parts. Nothing deploys, so it works the moment the drone leaves the ground.

07 · Drop test

Dropped on a headform.

Every design is compared with foam and with no padding, at equal weight and equal thickness.

The gap

Parachutes need height.

US rules limit flying a drone over people unless it meets an injury standard or has a waiver. Small-drone parachutes from AVSS and ParaZero list minimum heights of about 35–41 m. Takeoff and landing happen far below that.

Tested in the open.

Cheap foam is the benchmark. If the lattice can't beat foam at the same weight, it isn't worth building into a drone. We publish the method before the results.

Questions
01Is it certified or FAA compliant?

No. Nothing here is certified, and we don't claim it helps you meet any FAA requirement. We're building the test rig now and will publish what we find.

02Why not just use foam?

Foam is cheap and it works, which is why it's our benchmark. The lattice has to beat it at the same weight to be worth it. A printed part can also double as the prop guard and be tuned to one airframe.

03Does it replace a parachute?

No. Parachutes cover falls from higher up. The lattice covers the lowest few meters, where a parachute doesn't have room to open. They can work together.

04How much flight time does it cost?

We don't know yet, and it depends on the drone. Flight time lost is one of the numbers we'll measure and publish for every design.

05Do I need to send you a drone?

No. Mass, dimensions and CAD or photos of the underside and arms are enough to start. If you already use padding, a sample helps.
